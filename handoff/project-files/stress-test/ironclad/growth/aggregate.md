## Ironclad Review: Growth and revenue plan

Dispatching 6 lenses via subagent mode.

**Cycle:** 1 · **Type:** plan · **Lenses:** 6 completed / 0 failed (adversarial-review ran its five reviewer angles plus a 10th-Man inline, so their independence is reduced)

Lens key: AR adversarial-review · FP first-principles · CB cost-benefit · AM align-to-mission · PH plan-health-audit · PC precedent-check

Finding ids use R (revenue) so they don't collide with the engine's workstream letters. Growth's change list for the other plans and the planner note (notes/growth-plan.md) were reviewed as part of the plan. There were no early findings for growth to fold in. The lenses agree the shape holds: free first, one paid tier chosen by where the edge lands, no paid ads, misses kept public, the derived-numbers rule. The problems are the gate, the timing of going public, the rights to the data, and a change list that loads the cutover.

### Blockers

- **R1 [critical] scope: the paid gate cannot be decided as written and has no exit** (all six). The decision row lists four checks, "Jobs for you" says five, and the fifth ("8+ weeks live inside backtest range") exists only in the planner note. "Edge" has no definition (same hole as E16). At the notification plan's 2-5 sent alerts a day, 8 weeks gives roughly 110-280 graded calls; split by tier and window, no cell reaches a usable sample, so the check passes almost anything or never resolves. 2,000 followers across Telegram and X counts people twice and measures audience, not willingness to pay. The lawyer and the price licence are actions, not conditions, and the lawyer step is circular; they come last although they are the cheapest to start and the most likely to say no. Nothing says what happens if the gate never passes.
  - Fix: three checks with pass rules written now. (1) The pre-registered edge rule from E16, met in the backtest report. (2) The live record, pooled across calls and sized by a power calculation rather than split by cell, holds up against the backtest, counted from when grading starts. (3) Willingness to pay, from a poll in the channel. Rights move out of the gate and earlier: email Alpaca now, read the post sources' terms before engine PR 2 (R3), and hold a lawyer's scoping call before the public launch (R5); the gate only requires their answers in hand. Time-box it: 90 days after the public launch, Chris picks one of three outcomes agreed now: stay free as a research project, pull the API tier forward, or wind down.
- **R2 [critical] scope: the channel and the permanent record go public at the cutover, before any evidence** (AM, CB, FP, PH, PC, AR). Launch step 3 makes the Telegram channel public and says "every call from then on stays up" at engine PR 8. Tiers, evidence and grading arrive at PR 10. If the report then shows no edge, the plan's "there is no public launch" cannot take back a public channel, a public domain and weeks of ungraded calls under a profile line that promises "graded in public". This is where growth turns E1 and E15 from a sequencing issue into a public one.
  - Fix: the channel stays invite-only (old subscribers moved in) until Chris has read the report. Grading and the insert-only record run privately from the cutover, and the launch publishes them with their original timestamps. A daily hash of new first revisions goes to a public GitHub repo as the outside receipt (E20).
- **R3 [critical] dependencies: the money path has no cleared post source and no cleared price rights, and the site would republish the whole feed** (FP, CB, PH, PC, AM, AR). Posts: the CC0 CNN copy on GitHub stopped in October 2025; the engine reads the live ix.cnn.io file for both the history import and live alerts, so CC0 covers neither. That file's terms are unknown (growth says they bar commercial use, the project's own research found no published terms; both unverified). trumpstruth states no terms, which is not permission. The "All posts" list, full post text, a public `/api/v1/signals` change feed and the raw payload on each row together make a free real-time copy of Trump's feed, against the plan's own "we quote a line and link" (W10). Prices: whether Alpaca's free data may be published even as derived % moves is unverified, and the plan asks only "at the gate". Its own reason for hiding entry prices ("a % series plus one price rebuilds the prices") also condemns its minute-by-minute "% since the post" chart, and PR 10's outcome revisions would carry the entry price into the public alert (E14). The gate checks prices but not posts. Publicity also makes the free mirrors more likely to close.
  - Fix: before engine PR 2 and PR 3, read and record each source's terms in a one-row-per-source register (source, terms link, date checked, commercial use yes/no/unknown) and email CNN, trumpstruth's operator and Alpaca. Import history up to October 2025 from the CC0 copy so most of the backtest base is clean whatever CNN says. An excerpt-only rule for post text on public surfaces, decided before N2 and D1 freeze the public schemas. Until Alpaca answers in writing, public pages show the move at each fixed window, the move against the benchmark and hit or miss, with no minute-level chart. Keep ScrapeCreators switchable as a third source and run a daily coverage check before the public launch (E6). Post-feed rights go into the lawyer's scope.
- **R4 [critical] scope: the change list overbuilds and lands on the cutover path** (all six; CB and AR rate it critical). About 20 items go into the other plans "inside a PR the plan already has", which in practice means D1, N1 and N2, before any evidence or audience. Growth causes most of W4's D1 load, four of W8's page types, W11, W13 and W19. It repeats April 2026, when about five subscriber features shipped in two days for an audience of about five, and all 5,366 lines of `notifications/` are now being deleted. About 80% of the value sits in five items: the name and domain, follow buttons and one /about page, the derived-numbers rule, the report as the launch, and the Telegram channel the notification plan already builds.
  - Fix: split it into four buckets before sending.
    - At the cutover: domain and canonical links; follow buttons; one /about page with the methodology, disclaimers and correction policy; the derived-numbers rule naming the `alert.v1` fields it covers, as a schema test before engine PR 6; the excerpt rule; alert posts that are never edited; plain text preview tags on signal pages.
    - At the public launch: a static report page; ticker pages folded into the evidence view as a filter; a sitemap of called signals, ticker views and the report; X built once in N5; the rate-limiter fix and a load test (R11).
    - At the gate: Whop; the per-destination delay (applied to the API, site, change feed and MCP too); a tier column on api_keys instead of users and entitlements tables.
    - Cut: topic pages (a filter instead); the engine's report export (publish the frozen report); the monthly report and "then monthly" reposts; X daily results; the audience_daily table and invite-link attribution in code (Chris reads each link's join count in the Telegram app); ?ref on t.me and x.com links; generated preview images; the Telegram post as the "receipt"; public post links in the alert contract; the automated Monday scorecard; the MCP Registry listing under our domain; the prediction-market audience row.

### Risks

- **R5 [major] security: the legal steps come too late and the paid tier leans on the weakest part of the publisher exclusion** (all six). The "Tokyo Joe, $29 a month, 2000" price anchor and "SEC v. Park (2000), a $29-a-month email alert service" look like the same defendant, so the anchor is probably the adverse case (verify). Branch A ("edge within 15 minutes": paid members get alerts first) sells timing, which the plan says strains the exclusion, contradicts its own "we can't win on speed", and would leak through the public API, change feed, site and MCP anyway. Telegram's ad share is arguably compensation from day one and puts ads Telegram chooses, possibly brokers or exchanges, beside the calls. "Trade only after the public alert" does not by itself answer the scalping pattern the plan cites. "Direct messages" in branch B conflicts with the day-one rule against personal messages. Crypto brings commodity-adviser rules into scope. There is no entity or tax plan.
  - Fix: a one-hour scoping call with a securities lawyer before the public launch, not at the gate, covering the free phase, both branches, post-feed rights and Chris's own trading. Don't claim the ad share (count it as $0). Drop branch A unless the lawyer says it fits; default to branch B (filters, webhooks, API limits). Drop "direct messages" and the Tokyo Joe anchor.
- **R6 [major] scope: the follower thesis is untested and the plan has no checkpoint or stop line** (all six). The old system had a public graded record for months with no follow link, no analytics and about 5 subscribers, so "the record is the marketing" has never been tried here. The only measured accuracy is 42.9% on 501 calls at 7 days, direction only, so "no edge" is the base case. Alerts land 4-9 minutes after the headline accounts. The no-edge branch cancels the launch and discards the one asset that works without an edge: the descriptive findings journalists, researchers and developers can use either way.
  - Fix: state the starting audience. Publish the report whether or not there is an edge, with claims sized to the result; only the paid tier waits on an edge. Write the no-edge launch now (a research tracker). Set checkpoints for Chris to approve, for example 300 channel members 30 days after launch, 700 at 90, 1,000 at 180, read from named invite links in the Telegram app; missing them triggers the time-box in R1.
- **R7 [major] data-integrity: public claims must be measured and the record must be checkable** (PC, PH, AM, FP, AR). "Alerts in 2 to 4 minutes" rests on three posts while CNN's file refreshed every 8-26 minutes and the engine's target is p95 under 10. "Graded in public" would show from the cutover though grading starts at PR 10. "Every Trump post that moves markets" claims coverage the record cannot show. The Telegram post is edited in place, so it proves a time, not the content. The old system's record has known grading bugs. Nothing says what happens when a grading bug is found after calls are public.
  - Fix: the tagline uses the shadow week's measured p50; "graded in public" appears once outcome revisions flow; the signal page shows revision 1 as sent and `/alerts/{id}/revisions` is public; results go out as replies and a daily post, never edits (M3); a correction policy on /about; the old record is excluded from "live" and from the gate (E18).
- **R8 [major] scope: X is built twice and costs more than its cap** (CB, FP, AR). Both halves of the X rollout wait on the same edge, so the split changes nothing. Daily results repeat N5's 1-day replies and need live grading. A post with a link costs $0.20, so linked alert posts alone run about $12-30 a month against a $10 cap (X prices unverified).
  - Fix: one X build (N5) at the public launch after "edge" is defined; Chris posts the report thread by hand; no daily-results posts; verify X pricing before N5.
- **R9 [major] cost: the $250 has no time horizon and the cost of the first paid dollar sits outside it** (CB, PH, FP, AM, AR). Engine Railway and X are priced for three months; the gate is realistically 6-12 months out. The lawyer ($500-1,500), any price licence (unpriced) and forming an entity all come before the first dollar.
  - Fix: add a monthly running-cost line and a "cost to first paid dollar" line. Chris decides whether the $250 covers them.
- **R10 [major] dependencies: about 15 outside facts have no owner or date, and the plan assigned them to a stress test that cannot browse** (all six). Those that change what gets built, in the order they bite: CNN and trumpstruth terms (before engine PR 2); Alpaca on derived numbers and commercial use (before PR 3); Telegram invite-link counts and ad terms (before the cutover); X prices and automation rules (before N5); the competitor "no graded record" claim (before launch copy); Lowe, Park and commodity-adviser rules (lawyer, before the public launch); price-licence cost (before approving the money roadmap); Whop's seller policy (at the gate).
  - Fix: a verification list with an owner and a "before step N" date for each, worked in one browser sitting by Chris or by a thread whose network allows those hosts, with results written into the plan as "checked on <date>".
- **R11 [major] performance: the launch spike meets the known rate-limiter bug** (FP, AM, CB, PC, AR). A Show HN, Reddit post and X thread on the same day, with every tab polling every 5 seconds, against a limiter keyed on Railway's proxy (`api/rate_limit.py:10`, W6, M14): either every visitor gets a 429 or the spike passes straight to Neon.
  - Fix: the limiter fix plus a load test before Launch step 4; serve the report as a static, cached page; slower polling for anonymous readers.

### Gaps

- **R12 [major] scope: no risk register, no success measures besides the gate, no sizes and no dates** (PH, AR). Chris's own time (posting, drafts, invite links, any weekly summary) is the real limit on a no-ads plan and is not estimated. Fix: an eight-row risk table (no edge; source terms; follower stall; lawyer says no; X or Telegram limits; Reddit or HN removal; Whop; Chris's time), a size and home PR on each change row, and the earliest paid date.
- **R13 [minor] scope: small duplications** (PH). The Telegram channel is created in both the growth and notification job lists; it is one channel. Domain and DNS sit inside the cutover sitting; do them in the shadow week against a holding page. "The per-destination delay already does this" overstates what the notification plan has.
- **R14 [minor] scope: the earnings table has no downside row** (PC, AR). At 0.5-1% of 2,000 paying it is about $230-470 a month before the lawyer and licence; branch B's assumed 2-5% is optimistic.

### Counter-plan worth knowing (AR)

A "Trump Market Scoreboard": real-time calls go privately to Chris; public pages publish each call and its outcome after the fact, with the call's existence timestamped publicly at once (a hash or a "call logged" post); the research report launches either way; first money is a non-broker sponsor. It wins if there is no edge and removes most adviser and rights exposure, but gives up the "called in minutes" hook and reverses Chris's approved public Telegram alerts. Worth taking from it: the research launch regardless of edge, dropping branch A, a sponsor as a first-revenue candidate, unedited alert posts.

### Cuts and deferrals (overbuilding)

- The cut bucket in R4.
- users and entitlements tables until the gate; Whop already gates the paid Telegram channel (CB).
- Telegram ad share (R5); the price anchors, Whop/Stripe/Stars comparison and earnings table move into the gate memo (FP).
- Developer and agent marketing (MCP Registry, README as landing page) until N4 has users (CB, PH).

### Questions

- Is "Shitpost Alpha" final? Registering the domain and handles is the last free moment to rename, and newsrooms, web filters and some platforms filter the word (CB, FP, AM, AR).
- Does the engine stay open source? The repo is public under MIT (checked). If it stays open, the paid tier sells the record and the running service, not the code (FP, PH, CB).
- Does the $250 cover the lawyer and a price licence, or do they sit outside it (AM, PH, FP)?
- How many old subscribers are active, silenced by the old error counter, or blocked, and has `TELEGRAM_WEBHOOK_SECRET` been set since PR #241 (2026-08-21)? If not, `/start` has returned 403 since then (PC, PH).
- Which evidence layer and cost level must the launch edge show, and what alert delay does the backtest assume (PH, AR; see E5, E12, E16)?
- Will the channel have comments or a discussion group, and how are "what should I do?" questions handled under the no-personal-advice rule (AR)?

### Kept (the lenses agree these hold)

Win on evidence, not speed; free until a gate; one paid tier chosen by where the edge lands; no paid ads; Chris posts to HN and Reddit himself; the publisher rules from day one (same message to everyone, misses kept, disclaimers, operator-positions notice, X "Automated" label, coins named but no exchange); quote and link rather than resell posts; no referral links; the derived-numbers rule; Whop over Stripe and Stars; marking unverified claims in the text; the budget arithmetic ($105 + $35 + $15 = $155 of $250; $23.28 net per Whop member).

**Not converged:** 4 blockers, 8 major risks/gaps open; decision forks open: public channel at cutover, publishing with no edge, legal timing and ad share, branch A, whether $250 covers the money path, name, open source.
