# Counter-Planner: a full alternative on the opposite assumptions

Reviewer 5 of 5, run inline.

## Opposite assumptions
| The plan assumes | The counter-plan assumes |
| --- | --- |
| Growth comes from real-time public calls, graded | Growth comes from research and a scoreboard; the real-time calls stay private |
| Money comes from a signal subscription after an edge | Money comes from the media product (sponsor, newsletter) whether or not there is an edge, and Chris's own trading |
| Speed matters a little; evidence is the moat | Speed is irrelevant; after-the-fact analysis is the moat |
| Legal risk is deferred to a gate | Legal risk is designed out: commentary on past events only |

## The counter-plan: "Trump Market Scoreboard"
1. **Private engine.** The engine alerts only Chris (admin chat) and his own agents, through N1 and the API with a key. This is the mission's "a signal engine I'd actually trade on". It never publishes a call before or as it trades. There is no public head start, and nothing to delay or protect.
2. **Public scoreboard, after the fact.** Each post with a market call gets a public page about 30-60 minutes later, or at the next close: "what the engine said, what happened, vs SPY". This is commentary on past events, the clearest ground in the plan's own Lowe reading (impersonal, not timed to the market). The record still includes every call and every miss.
3. **The launch is the research report,** posted whether there is an edge or not. "Do Trump's posts move markets? Every post since 2022, graded" is a story either way, and journalists cite either answer.
4. **One channel, one cadence.** The Telegram channel carries the daily "posts and what moved" digest and the Monday scorecard. X is posted by hand from drafts at no API cost.
5. **Money.** A sponsor for the weekly scorecard or report once readership exists, with sponsors that are not brokers or exchanges. Separately, Chris trades on the private engine. A paid tier is considered only if the private record shows an edge, and then with the lawyer, as in the plan.
6. **What gets cut from the other plans:** branch A's delay, X automation (N5) until a per-alert X decision, invite-link attribution, `audience_daily`, the topic, ticker and report page types (use Evidence filters and a static report), per-page preview images, and the MCP Registry listing.

## What it gets right that the plan does not
- It wins in the likely no-edge world: the research and scoreboard still attract readers.
- Most of the adviser question goes away, because there are no public real-time calls.
- Chris's own trading conflict goes away: he can trade before anything is published, because nothing published is a call.
- Display and post-feed rights shrink. The posts shown are hours old, as excerpts, and the moves are window results, not minute series.
- Build size drops further than any finding in this review asks.

## What it gives up
- The "called in minutes" hook, and with it the retail traders who want live calls. That is the audience most likely to pay for a signal product.
- The public out-of-sample proof made in real time. A scoreboard published after the fact can be accused of hindsight, unless the private calls are timestamped publicly at once: a hash or a one-line "call logged" post with no direction shown. That is cheap and keeps the proof.
- Owner fit: Chris approved real-time public alerts on Telegram in the notification plan (notifications.txt:385-387).

## Synthesis worth taking into the plan
- Make the research report the launch whether or not there is an edge.
- Drop branch A.
- Make a sponsor, not a subscription, the first candidate revenue line, with no broker sponsors before the lawyer.
- Keep public real-time alerts, but publish them as unedited messages, so they work as receipts, with results in a daily digest.
