## Blockers

- **[critical] scope**: The soft launch at cutover (Launch step 3, PR 8) starts a public, permanent, ungraded record before any evidence exists. Step 3 makes the Telegram channel public and says "the public record starts that day, and every call from then on stays up". The engine plan says alerts carry "evidence is still being built" until PR 10, and outcome revisions (the grading) only begin at PR 10. So from PR 8 to PR 10 the channel publishes bare LLM calls that cannot yet be graded, under a profile line that promises "graded in public" and a rule that misses stay up. This contradicts the plan's own sentence "nothing is promoted before the evidence is in". If the PR 10 report then shows no edge, a public record of unevidenced v0 calls already exists and cannot be taken back. It also starts the gate's "8+ weeks live" clock on a product that is not the evidence product. This is where growth causes the earlier "edge gate sits too late" and "live grading waits on PR 10" findings.
  - **Recommendation:** Keep the channel private (invite link only, which the notification plan already allows) from cutover; send old subscribers the invite link. Flip it to a public username, move the site to the new domain, start the public record, and start the gate clock at PR 10, on launch day. The profile line "graded in public" goes live only once grading exists. Zero extra build; it removes risk and the Telegram ad share (R3) at the same stroke.

- **[critical] scope**: The paid gate cannot be evaluated as written, so approving "When money starts" approves an undefined test.
  - The plan text names four conditions in the decision row, then refers to "the first two checks" and "all five checks" without listing them. The thresholds (8+ weeks live, 2,000 followers across Telegram and X) exist only in the planner's note and in a drawn diagram the export shows as a placeholder.
  - "An edge" is used three ways and never defined: the public launch (backtest edge after costs), per-alert X posts ("still wait for an edge", which is vacuous if launch already required an edge), and the gate ("an edge in the live record").
  - Measurable? On the notification plan's own volume (2-5 MEDIUM/HIGH alerts a day), 8 weeks gives roughly 110-280 alerts. No topic x ticker x window cell reaches the engine's own 20-case floor, let alone survives its Benjamini-Hochberg correction. A single pooled hit rate would need roughly 56-61% (Wilson 95%) just to clear a 50% placebo. If "edge" is judged by eyeballing the dashboard's cells, the gate can pass on forking paths while goal 3 (an edge Chris would trade on) is unserved.
  - Order and time: with the clock fixed per the blocker above, the earliest gate is PR 10 plus 8 weeks, after a 12-PR build, and 2,000 followers must arrive from a standing start inside that window with no ads. There is no checkpoint before then. The Unusual Whales precedent (18k to 2.4M over five years) is one outlier; I also believe it is a staffed company, not "one person" (unverified).
  - The followers count (Telegram plus X) double-counts overlapping people and has no definition of active or retained.
  - If one never passes: only "no edge, so rethink" is covered. Nothing for a backtest edge that decays live, followers that stall at 1,200, counsel saying "register as an adviser", or a price licence that costs more than the revenue. No date-box exists, so the growth machinery (domain, pages, scorecards) could be maintained indefinitely for a paid tier that never opens.
  - **Recommendation:** Replace the gate paragraph with a table: condition | one pre-registered measure | data source | earliest date | what happens if it never passes. Fix the measure before cutover: for example, sent alerts only, one chosen window, abnormal return against the placebo, n of at least 150, Wilson lower bound above the placebo rate and above costs, and live results inside the backtest interval. Start the clock at PR 10. Define followers as Telegram members plus X followers minus a hand-estimated overlap, held for 30 days. Add a mid-course checkpoint at launch plus 4 weeks (extrapolate from ?ref and invite-link joins) and a hard review date, for example launch plus 26 weeks, where Chris picks one of: stay free as a research asset, restructure as a developer/API product, or stop. Define "edge" once and name the three places that use it.

## Risks

- **[major] security**: "Free is the safe phase, and the publisher exclusion carries the paid phase" rests on unverified law and contradicts itself in four places. Every case and reading here comes from search summaries.
  - Money in the free phase: the Telegram ad share (50% of ad revenue past 1,000 subscribers, paid in TON) is the plan's only free-phase income, and it is compensation tied to a channel of specific-ticker calls. The plan calls this phase "the safest legally" while turning that income on. At 1,000-2,000 subscribers it is pocket change.
  - Price anchor against precedent: "Tokyo Joe" at $29 a month (2000) is the price anchor. As I recall, and cannot check from here, the SEC v. Park defendant was that same Tokyo Joe service. If so, the plan's price anchor and its adverse precedent are one case, and it turned on market-timed alerts, which is what a fast-edge tier sells.
  - The "not timed to specific market activity" element is, as I recall, from the SEC's 1987 interpretive release and later cases, not Lowe. The plan attributes the whole test to Lowe. This matters for what the lawyer is asked: event-fired alerts are the whole product, not an edge case.
  - Variant exposure: "paid gets alerts first, public waits" is the most exposed design (event-timed, differential timing, backtest results as marketing). The slow-edge design (filters, webhooks, limits) is the least. The plan picks the variant from where the edge lands, not from which one counsel allows. The slow-edge list also includes "direct messages", which the day-one rule bans (see Questions).
  - The lawyer ($500-1,500, a rough guess) arrives only at the gate, after the public alerts have run for months. If the exclusion fails, the paid tier must be restructured or the operator registers. The plan does not say which product wins in that case.
  - **Recommendation:** Do not enable or withdraw the Telegram ad share until counsel says so (verify whether it can be switched off); the cost of waiting is about zero. Buy a one-hour consult before the public launch on the free-phase posture: public ticker calls, ad share, disclaimers, operator trades, the backtest numbers used as marketing. At the gate, scope the full review to both tier variants, the source terms (next finding) and the Whop flow. State now that if counsel rejects event-timed alerts, the paid tier is the slow-edge product. Verify the Lowe/Park reading before the soft launch, and verify the Tokyo Joe link before using $29 as an anchor.
