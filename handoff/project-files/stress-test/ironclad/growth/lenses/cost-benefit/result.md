## Risks

- **[major] scope: The paid gate's five checks don't work as a gate as written** (Money, "The gate"; the decision row Chris approves names four checks, notes/growth-plan.md names five). (1) A backtest edge after costs can only be measured once "edge" is defined (E16). It is the right first check. (2) "8+ weeks live inside backtest range" doesn't define "inside range". At the notification plan's 2-5 sent alerts a day, 8 weeks gives about 110-280 graded calls. A confidence interval that wide overlaps almost any backtest number, so the check passes trivially; split by tier and window, it never reaches a usable sample. It also can't start until live grading exists, which waits on PR 10 (W1), so money starts no earlier than PR 10 plus 8 weeks. (3) 2,000 followers across Telegram and X can be counted, but it measures audience rather than willingness to pay, counts people who follow on both twice, and comes with no expected timeline. The only support offered is one success story (Unusual Whales). (4) The lawyer and (5) licensed prices come last, although they are the checks most likely to answer "no" or "not this product", and they are the cheapest to start. The Alpaca letter costs $0. A display-licensed feed has no price anywhere in the plans and could be a monthly fee larger than the 2% revenue row. No check says what happens if it never passes: there is no time limit and no fallback.
  - **Recommendation:** Define checks 1 and 2 as numbers fixed in advance, for example a live hit rate whose lower bound beats the placebo on 30 or more sent alerts in the primary cell, net of 20 bp. Replace check 3 with a free test of demand: a waitlist or a Telegram poll ("would you pay $25 a month for ..."), triggered at about 40 committed people, the plan's own 2%-of-2,000 row. That number is also what pays for the lawyer. Send the Alpaca letter now, since D1's public % moves need the answer too. Price a display-licensed feed before Chris approves the money roadmap. Add a review date: if the gate hasn't passed N months after the public launch, stop the recurring growth spend and keep the free channel running unattended.

- **[major] security: The plan's price anchor is the court case that went against that model, and the "edge within 15 minutes" variant is the most exposed** (Money Step 2, "Price anchors"; "Stay a publisher, not an adviser"). The anchor ("'Tokyo Joe' charged $29 a month for email stock alerts in 2000") and the adverse precedent ("In SEC v. Park (2000) a court found a $29-a-month email alert service was not a bona fide publication") have the same price, year and product. From memory, Park is the Tokyo Joe case (verify). The fast-edge variant, where paid members get alerts first and the public waits, sells advice timed to events, on the strength of that timing. That is exactly the Lowe condition the plan says event alerts strain. Separately, a person counts as an investment adviser under the Advisers Act only if paid. Taking Telegram's ad share on the alert channel in Step 1 may count as payment (a question for the lawyer), so "free until the gate" is not clean if the share is claimed. Crypto alerts also bring the CFTC's commodity-adviser rules into the lawyer's scope, which the plan doesn't list.
  - **Recommendation:** Don't claim Telegram ad revenue before the lawyer's review. By the plan's own account it is small; also check whether the channel can switch ads off. Drop Tokyo Joe as a price anchor. Make the variant the lawyer's first question: timed paid-first alerts, or tools and data (filters, webhooks, API limits). Add CFTC adviser rules for crypto and the operator-trading rule to the same review. Prefer the tools-and-data variant unless the lawyer clears timing. A short, scoped consult before the gate may cost less than learning at the gate that the planned product can't be sold.

- **[major] scope: X at the launch means three kinds of posts on top of a PR that already exists, and the edge that holds back per-alert posts is the same edge that gates the launch** (decision "X"; channel table; Notifications row). The public launch needs the backtest to "show an edge after costs". N5's per-alert posts wait until "the backtest shows an edge after costs". Read literally, both start on the same day, so "per-alert posts still wait for an edge" either changes nothing or refers to some other edge (a live one?) that the plan never defines. As written, the notification planner builds X twice: first for content posts (report, daily results, scorecard), then for per-alert posts. Daily results need live grading (W1) and repeat the 1-day result replies N5 already plans. The Monday scorecard brings back something the notification plan dropped on purpose (the old `/scorecard` in `notifications/telegram_bot.py`, which PR 11 deletes). The growth loop's "every alert links to its own signal page" doesn't hold on X either: a post with a link costs $0.20, so the alert posts alone would cost about $12-30 a month at 2-5 alerts a day, over the $10 cap. X's pay-per-use prices are unverified. If X only sells a fixed monthly tier, automated posting doesn't fit in the $250.
  - **Recommendation:** Define "edge" once, and use that definition for the launch, N5 and the gate. Build N5 once, at the launch: per-alert posts with 1-day result replies, as approved, plus one weekly scorecard text that also goes to the Telegram channel. The site's Record page is the scorecard. Cut daily results. Chris posts the report thread by hand from the X app, which is free and needs no developer app. Check X's API prices and free allowance before N5 is written; if pay-per-use doesn't exist, X stays manual.

- **[major] scope: The $250 has no time horizon, and the money roadmap runs past the 3 months the other plans budgeted** (Budget; engine Budget "Always-on engine on Railway, 3 months"; notifications "X at its cap for three months"). Railway for the engine ($15-30) and X ($10 a month cap) are priced for 3 months. The gate can't come before PR 10, plus 8 weeks, plus 2,000 followers, plus the lawyer. After month 3, Railway, X, live model calls, the domain and Neon (E21) keep costing money with no budget line. Check 5's licensed feed may be a recurring cost that no budget includes. "About $95 left" holds only for three months.
  - **Recommendation:** Add the monthly running cost after month 3 and the month in which the $250 runs out at that rate. Tie it to the review date in the gate finding: if the gate hasn't passed by then, stop X and other optional spend. Price check 5 before approval.

- **[major] scope: Nobody has tested whether people will follow, and the plan builds the growth surfaces before testing it** ("Who it's for and why they'd follow us"; "How people find us"; Launch step 4, "If the report shows no edge"). The positioning rests on three things. The first is a competitor claim from search summaries: "none of them publishes a graded record against a baseline". The second is one success story, Unusual Whales, which is a different product. The third is a record that will mostly read LOW (E5) and arrive minutes after the headline accounts: the median target is under 4 minutes, and E12 puts a typical wait nearer 8.6 minutes. People who trade on speed won't follow an alert that is 4-9 minutes late. They might follow for the evidence line, but that line is weak until tiers exist. After a backtest edge is picked out of many tests, the live record will probably fall back toward chance (winner's curse, costs, latency), and it will do so in public from day one. Nothing after the launch decides whether to keep investing, and the no-edge outcome is "rethought" instead of being decided now.
  - **Recommendation:** Check the competitor claim by opening Trump Market Tracker, Trump Tracker and the TACO Index before writing launch copy (Launch step 4). Treat the public launch as the experiment. Build only the launch bucket, then set a 30-day threshold on followers and waitlist sign-ups before building any search pages, scorecard, MCP listing or developer marketing. Decide the no-edge branch now. The report goes out as research, since it is the asset journalists want either way and costs nothing extra. The channel stays free with no X spend, and no more growth features get built.
